## Chip count for the 250–700 GB/s baseline

Per 32-bit channel: `bandwidth = pin_rate × 32 / 8`. One x32 device = one channel.

| Target | GDDR7 32 Gb/s (128 GB/s/chip) | GDDR7 28 Gb/s (112 GB/s/chip) | GDDR6 16 Gb/s (64 GB/s/chip) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 250 GB/s | 2 chips → 256 GB/s, 4 GB | 3 chips → 336 GB/s, 6 GB | 4 chips → 256 GB/s, 8 GB |
| 500 GB/s | 4 chips → 512 GB/s, 8 GB | 5 chips → 560 GB/s, 10 GB | 8 chips → 512 GB/s, 16 GB |
| 700 GB/s | 6 chips → 768 GB/s, 12 GB | 7 chips → 784 GB/s, 14 GB | 11 chips → 704 GB/s, 22 GB |

**Capacity is the binding constraint at the low end.** Raw Q4 matrix weights are about 5.95 GB (`python3 model/gemma4.py`), so two 2 GB devices cannot hold them. Three devices provide only 6 GB, which cannot fit those weights plus approximately 0.74 GB of FP16 group-32 scales, before KV or system overhead. At least four 2 GB devices are needed even for an 8 GB capacity starting point; actual context and concurrency requirements may require more.

Cost at observed spot prices: 6× GDDR7 16 Gb ≈ $10 (Alibaba) to $150 (Accio high quote); 11× GDDR6 ≈ $394–$458. Memory is not the dominant cost driver unless the high quotes are real.

## Should we focus on caches? No — for weights

- Weights per token (Q4): 5.95 GB must be streamed once per token. A 16 MB on-chip SRAM is 0.27% of that; even 64 MB is 1.1%. Caching cannot remove weight traffic — each dense weight is read once per token by definition.
- What caching *can* hold: activations, per-group scales, and near-term KV. KV at 4096 context is 0.20 GB/request (8-bit), 1.61 GB for batch 8 — too big for SRAM at batch, but the recurrent working set (last-token activations, scales) fits comfortably.
- The real lever for "bulk" is **batch amortisation**, not caching: weights are shared across requests, so 8 concurrent requests at 250 GB/s give 264 tok/s aggregate (33 tok/s each) vs 40.6 tok/s sequential. At 700 GB/s, batch 8 gives 626 tok/s aggregate.
- Long context hurts: at 262144 context, batch 8 KV is 18.5 GB/token, dropping aggregate to 81.7 tok/s at 250 GB/s. KV, not weights, dominates there.

**Design consequence:** size the DRAM interface and capacity together (at least 8 GB for the weights-plus-scales starting point), keep SRAM for activations/KV slices/scales, and investigate batch parallelism. The generated throughput estimate for `rtl/q4_gemv_tile.sv` assumes one group every six cycles with an always-ready 32-bit port: about 3.3 bytes/cycle, or 3.3 GB/s at an assumed 1 GHz. The resulting estimate of roughly 75–210 tiles for 250–700 GB/s excludes controller overhead, arbitration, area, timing closure, and power; it is not a measured or validated array design.
