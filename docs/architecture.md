# Factory inference chip: first architecture pass (2026-10-14)

> **Provenance:** this design note belongs to a test autonomously generated by Qwen-Flash-Next. It is a generated proposal, not an independently validated chip architecture. Performance figures are analytical ceilings; source and marketplace claims have not been independently revalidated for publication. See the [project overview](../README.md).

**Target:** Gemma 4 12B Unified **text decoder** initially. The official [config](https://huggingface.co/google/gemma-4-12B/resolve/main/config.json) specifies 48 layers, 3840 hidden, 16 query heads, 8 local KV heads × 256 dims, one global KV head × 512 dims, 40 sliding-window layers (1024 tokens), eight global layers, 15360 MLP width, tied 262144-vocabulary embeddings and 262144 maximum positions. Global K and V share a projection but remain distinct normalized/rotated cached values; see [Hugging Face projection implementation](https://github.com/huggingface/transformers/blob/main/src/transformers/models/gemma4/modeling_gemma4.py). This **does not** include multimodal prefill, model-specific norms/softcaps, biases, small scale tensors, KV padding, routing overhead, memory controller refresh, or interconnect contention. No final chip specification follows from a roofline alone.

## Compute and memory partition

- One in-order RISC-V control processor drives command queues, DMA, page translation/security boundary and interrupts; the accelerator is a **separate device**. `rtl/rv32i_core.sv` implements RV32I + Zicsr + Zifencei (M-mode only) and a small RVV 1.0 subset (VLEN=128, element-serial, 16 bytes/register) for control-side vector code and ISA bring-up; it is **not** the GEMV engine (one element per cycle cannot approach the 250–700 GB/s class) and is not full RVV. The weight-streaming engine is the separate Q4 SIMD tile below. Evidence so far: official riscv-tests rv32ui 42/42 and rv32mi 14/16 (the two misses are optional Sdtrig debug triggers and PMP; misaligned scalar accesses are now handled in hardware) plus hand-written RVV tests and a reference-checked Q4 tile bench (`sw/regress.sh`, 63 suites, 0 unexpected failures). **riscv-arch-test (ACT) now runs the full `rv32i/I` group: 39/39 PASS** via genuine Sail reference-signature comparison (`sw/act_regress.sh`), including fence/fence.i reserved-encoding cases. Not yet run: ACT `M`/`Za*`/`Zba`/vector/priv groups, an official RVV suite, formal verification, certification.
- 32-way SIMD Q4 unpack/sign extension and multiply/accumulate over groups of 32 plus per-group scale; BF16 inputs and FP32 accumulation remain a *proposed* tile contract. `rtl/q4_dot_product.sv` implements the signed Q4×INT8 → INT32 dot. `rtl/q4_gemv_tile.sv` additionally unpacks packed nibbles, applies signed Q16.16 per-group scales, and accumulates in 64-bit fixed point. Neither implements BF16/FP32 accumulation or DMA. The [Python packing reference](../sw/q4_gemv.py) uses floating-point scales and is not a bit-exact floating-point hardware oracle. Dense GEMM for prefill and reduction units for RMSNorm, attention softmax, p-RoPE and activation remain proposals; validate numerical quality before freezing microarchitecture.
- DMA streams tiled model weights from GDDR to local SRAM and reuses activations across output channels/batch. Keep activations, scales, and near-term KV working sets on chip where practical; long-context KV and all weights stay in GDDR. 6 GB Q4 model weights cannot fit a modest SRAM: caching a few MB only helps recurrent working sets, *not* streaming each dense weight once per token. Eight simultaneous decode requests amortize weight traffic; attention KV reads still scale with batch.
- Choose **GDDR6/7 external memory as provisional baseline**. Physical design must budget PHY/SerDes or hard IP, package pins, PCB layers, power/thermal, signal integrity, controller verification and ECC strategy. Speed on a DRAM data sheet is a pin-level peak, not achieved bandwidth. Do not commit to a tape-out memory type or price without manufacturer-supported supply quotes.

## Capacity and performance (ideal ceilings)

Run `python3 model/gemma4.py`. The text matrix estimate is ~11.91B params (official card rounds total to 11.95B); Q4 weights ~5.95 decimal GB **before scales, metadata, norms, alignment and other weights**. With a 32-weight block, FP16 scales alone add ~0.74 GB for ~11.9B weights (2 bytes / 32 weights). A 16 GB board holds one model plus KV and system overhead at modest concurrency; at 262144 positions, 8-bit KV is ~2.32 GB **per sequence**. Eight full-context sequences need ~18.52 GB of KV alone; a 16 GB board cannot fit them even before weights. The provisional Q4 format does not itself guarantee quality; evaluate group size and BF16/FP16 scale formats before selecting capacity.

| Peak GB/s | Example memory wiring | Peak Q4 single-stream tok/s at 4096 context | At 262144 context | Eight streams aggregate at 4096 context |
| ---: | --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 256 | 4 × 16 Gbit GDDR6 ×32 at 16 Gb/s = 8 GB | ~42 | ~31 | ~271 |
| 512 | 8 × 16 Gbit GDDR6 ×32 at 16 Gb/s = 16 GB | ~83 | ~62 | ~541 |
| 704 | 11 × 16 Gbit GDDR6 ×32 at 16 Gb/s = 22 GB | ~114 | ~85 | ~626 |

These are *theoretical* 100%-utilized BW and 16 effective TOPS ceilings; 704 GB/s is slightly above the requested 700 GB/s baseline, and 11 channels/352-bit bus is only arithmetic, **not** a recommended PCB topology. At 70% effective pin bandwidth and unchanged compute, the single-stream 512 GB/s/4096-context estimate becomes ~58 tok/s ceiling. At batch 8, 512 GB/s and 262144 context, KV scan dominates and aggregate ceiling falls to ~167 tok/s (~21 tok/s per stream), **but requires more capacity than this 16 GB example board provides**. Real prefill, decode scheduling, DRAM utilization and quantization kernel efficiency remain unmeasured. Four 24 Gbit ×32 GDDR7 devices at 28 Gb/s would offer 12 GB and 448 GB/s peak; eight offer 24 GB and 896 GB/s peak, if sourced/validated. Prefer 8× GDDR6 16Gb for **initial capacity/bandwidth exploration**, not fixed BOM: its 16 GB capacity cannot sustain eight maximum-context jobs.

## Multimodal prefill: 16 images + 40K-token prompt

12B Unified has **no vision encoder**: raw 16×16 patches are linearly projected into the decoder ([model card](https://huggingface.co/google/gemma-4-12B)). So every image token costs a full 12B decoder pass. The image token budget per image is selectable: 70/140/280/560/1120 (config default 280). 16 images at 280 tokens is 4,512 tokens. With 40K of text, one request is **44,512 tokens, about 1.26 PetaOps**, and **~0.53 GB of 8-bit KV** (the 5:1 sliding window keeps this small).

Prefill is **compute-bound**. Decode is bandwidth-bound. With chunked prefill (chunk ≥512 tokens), weights stream once per chunk, so DRAM traffic stops mattering: latency is the same at 250 and 700 GB/s. Ideal latency (`python3 model/gemma4.py`, 100% utilization):

| Request | 100 TOPS | 400 TOPS | 1000 TOPS |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 16 images @70 + 40K text, cold | 11.5 s | 2.9 s | 1.15 s |
| 16 images @280 + 40K text, cold | 12.6 s | 3.2 s | 1.26 s |
| 16 images @1120 + 40K text, cold | 17.4 s | 4.4 s | 1.74 s |
| 40K text prefix already cached, 16 images @280 new | 1.5 s | 0.38 s | 0.15 s |

So the existing 16 TOPS assumption is far too small for this workload: it would take about 79 s. **Sub-second cold prefill needs ≥~1.3 effective dense INT8 PetaOps**, which is GPU-class silicon. The compute array, not GDDR, becomes the sizing constraint. Chunk size sets the crossover. At 400 TOPS and 500 GB/s, 128-token chunks are still memory-bound (4.0 s vs 3.2 s compute). At ≥512 tokens, compute dominates. A 2048-token BF16 activation block is ~16 MB, so prefill needs that much on-chip SRAM (or tile it).

Methods, in order of leverage:
1. **Prefix KV caching.** Reuse the KV of a stable 40K prefix (system prompt, documents, instructions) across requests. Only the images and new text then get prefilled, cutting prefill work ~8×. Each cached 40K prefix costs ~0.50 GB of DRAM. Only works when the prefix really repeats, because a token's KV depends on everything before it.
2. **Smaller image budget.** @70 instead of @1120 saves ~34% of the ops in this request. Pick the budget per task: low for classification, high for OCR or reading small text.
3. **Size the MAC array for prefill.** Batched GEMM on the Q4 weights; intra-chunk K/V kept on chip. This is a different engine from the decode GEMV tile. The `q4_gemv_tile.sv` one-row-at-a-time datapath reuses no weights across tokens, so it cannot reach these numbers.
4. **Prefill/decode disaggregation.** Run prefill on a compute-heavy die or board and decode on a bandwidth-heavy one, then hand the ~0.53 GB KV across. Alternatively, **chunked prefill interleaved with decode** on one chip, so ongoing decodes don't stall while a 44K prompt is ingested.
5. **Lower precision or sparsity.** FP8/INT4 activation×weight MACs raise effective TOPS per mm²; model quality must be validated first.

**How many run in parallel.** All requests share **one weight copy** (~5.95 GB Q4 + ~0.74 GB FP16 group-32 scales), so concurrency is limited by KV capacity, not by "model copies". At 44,512 tokens and 8-bit KV, with 0.5 GB reserved:

| DRAM | 8 GB | 12 GB | 16 GB | 24 GB | 32 GB |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Concurrent resident requests | 1 | 9 | 16 | 31 | 46 |

Decode ceilings at this context (Q4, 400 TOPS), aggregate / per-stream tok/s:

| GB/s | batch 1 | batch 4 | batch 8 | batch 16 |
| ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 250 | 38.5 / 38.5 | 124 / 31 | 196 / 24 | 276 / 17 |
| 500 | 77 / 77 | 247 / 62 | 392 / 49 | 553 / 35 |
| 700 | 108 / 108 | 346 / 87 | 548 / 69 | 774 / 48 |

Prefill throughput bounds how many *new* requests per second the chip can accept. At 400 TOPS, a cold request takes ~3.2 s of compute, so ~0.3 cold requests/s per chip. Running two separate 12B models on one chip would need two weight copies (~13.4 GB) and would halve the KV left for sequences. Prefer one model at high batch. Separate models (e.g. a Qwen-style draft for speculative decoding) are a capacity line item, not free.

## Model flexibility and milestone

Qwen-style n-grams/speculative decoding are **model/post-training and draft-verification** decisions, not a hardware instruction requirement yet. Keep queues and batch scheduler flexible for draft/verify workloads; do not hardwire draft model dimensions. Gemma 4's published config is the reference, while a hybrid retrained checkpoint will have different tensor shapes/KV policy and must be profiled separately. Next gate: validate Q4 numerical quality; choose latency vs tokens/s vs concurrent context capacity, sustainable board-level bandwidth/power and vendor memory quotes; then implement vector datapath + DMA and compare a real kernel against this roofline. Model costs and factory scale cannot be responsibly fixed without board/node/process, power envelope, yield and volume.
